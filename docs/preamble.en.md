# LACUNA — Preamble

## Conformism as Infrastructure

Two phenomena share the same mechanism.

In comment threads across news sites, social networks, and forums, a precise observation made by an unknown person vanishes under the weight of hostile reactions or collective silence. In a piece of institutional skeptic writing, a study published in a peer-reviewed journal is absent — not refuted, absent. In both cases, what operates is not error. It is organized conformism.

---

## 1. The Appeal to Conformism

The *spiral of silence* (Noelle-Neumann, 1974) establishes that individuals who perceive their opinions as minority positions tend to stay silent out of fear of social isolation. This mechanism has grown more complex online: algorithms amplify dominant positions, distorting the perception of what the majority actually thinks. People willing to raise a question in private will not do so in a comment section where they anticipate group hostility.

In institutional skeptic discourse, the same appeal operates in a more polished form: *"it's complicated," "experts agree," "you don't have the tools to judge."* These formulations delegate epistemic authority to an external source while insinuating that doubt is a form of naivety or social danger. The result is identical: the interlocutor abandons autonomous evaluation.

---

## 2. Silence About Certain Things

The other mechanism is structured omission. In a comment thread, it takes the form of algorithmic invisibility: a well-founded objection exists, on page four, with two upvotes. In institutional skeptic discourse, it takes the form of declared non-existence: *"no serious study has shown"* — a claim that is often false and rarely verified by the audience.

Neither form leaves a visible trace. A refuted argument is present in the debate. An ignored argument is not. This is precisely why this type of suppression has resisted systematic treatment until now.

---

## 3. Recurring Operations

Both environments also share:

- **Guilt by association**: disqualification by proximity without engaging the content
- **Emotional appeal**: serious cases, victims, impending catastrophes that short-circuit evaluation through affective saturation
- **Asymmetry of the burden of proof**: doubt must justify itself; consensus is treated as a natural state exempt from demonstration
- **Credential disqualification**: competence in logic treated as an acquired specialty rather than an exercised faculty
- **Accumulation of peripheral objections** that buries a central objection under a volume discouraging any methodical response

---

## 4. The Institutionalization of Skepticism and Contamination Smearing

For a long time, the grey zones of knowledge lived in their own networks, relatively insulated from public debate. A parapsychology researcher could work in a government laboratory, publish in specialized journals, have peers and critics — and remain invisible to the general public. A political scientist documenting phenomena of institutional collusion worked in their corner, read by a few hundred people. A clinic exploring unorthodox medicine accumulated data that could be found if one looked, without anyone feeling compelled to render a public verdict.

That compartmentalization broke down with the massification of information. These researchers, findings, and practices became visible — sometimes through mainstream documentaries, sometimes by reaching general awareness through accumulated evidence. It was precisely at that moment that institutional skepticism mobilized, bringing with it a new logic: contamination smearing. Since these subjects had become visible enough to threaten consensus, it was no longer sufficient to refute them — it became necessary to make the very act of mentioning them toxic. A serious political scientist who appears in a media outlet deemed undesirable gets filed in the same bin as the outlet that hosts them. Someone who honestly cites the work of a consciousness research laboratory — published, peer-reviewed, funded — finds themselves associated with esotericists, conspiracy theorists, and revisionists, depending on the rhetorical moment.

The result is a zone of prohibition that has expanded considerably over twenty years. Questions that were open to academic debate are today subject to a de facto ban: not because they have been refuted, but because raising them has become a sign of suspect allegiance.

---

## 5. What Computational Power Changes

These mechanisms are not new. What changes is that they can now be detected at scale, with verifiable sources, across corpora that no human team could process in the same timeframe. Auditing one source was within reach of a motivated team. Doing it for hundreds of sources simultaneously, with real-time verification against global scientific literature databases, was not.

What protected conformist discourse was not its solidity. It was the disproportion between institutional production capacity and individual response capacity. Lacuna reduces that disproportion.

---

## 6. Lacuna Takes a Side

To enter a debate where the currency of exchange consists of facts and their interpretation is necessarily to defend a conception of truth. Claiming neutrality in this context is not an epistemic position — it is a rhetorical posture, most often in service of the status quo.

Lacuna takes the side of examining facts against their suppression. Of questions asked against their prohibition. Of rigorous refutation against convenient omission. This position is not ideological in the partisan sense: it is methodological. It holds that truth is better found in a space where objections circulate than in a space where they are smothered before being heard.

That is the only side Lacuna defends — and it defends it explicitly.

---

*Its adversary is not science. It is conformism disguised as science.*
